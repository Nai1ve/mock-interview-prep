# Interview Stages — Questioning Patterns & Assessment Focus

> The interviewer's mental model is different at each stage. Using the same answer set across all stages is a common failure mode.

## Stage 1: HR Screen (phone / video, 30 min)

**Interviewer**: HR / Talent Acquisition / Recruiter

**Mental model:**
- Can I filter out the "obviously wrong" candidates (saving hiring manager time)?
- Is this candidate actually interested in the role (or just spamming applications)?
- Salary expectation, location, start date — if these are off, do not waste time.

**Most common questions:**
- Self-introduction (1-2 minute version)
- Why leaving / why this role
- Salary expectation / location / start date
- Resume question: what about [that employment gap / that short stint]
- How did you hear about us / what other companies are you talking to

**HR does not dig into technical detail** (unless this is a technical HR). The focus is "soft signals": clarity of speech, professional polish, self-positioning awareness.

**Signs you aced this stage:**
- 1-minute self-intro precisely positions: "I am X, I have done Y, I'm looking for Z-type roles"
- Reason for leaving does not bad-mouth the previous employer (even when there was real fault on their side)
- Salary expectation has a concrete anchor (avoid vague "depends on the company")

## Stage 2: Hiring manager / direct supervisor (45-60 min)

**Interviewer**: your future manager or senior peer

**Mental model:**
- Can this person help solve my team's pain over the next 3-6 months?
- Will their working style mesh with mine (communication, decision-making, self-direction)?
- The projects on their resume — did *they* do them, or did the team do them and the candidate piggybacked?

**Most common questions:**
- **Resume deep-dive**: pick 1-2 projects to dissect (context → decisions → execution → outcome)
- **Decision replay**: why did you pick A over B (testing depth of thinking)
- **Hard situations**: what was the project's biggest challenge / have you had a conflict with a stakeholder
- **"If I asked you to do X, walk me through your first week"** — testing hands-on capability
- Industry awareness — biggest trend / biggest risk in this space right now

**Hiring managers chase detail.** Be ready to explain the methodology behind every quantitative number (not "we did X, it improved Y%", but "I used method X to reach Y because of Z, ran into W on the way, here's how I solved it").

**Signs you aced this stage:**
- For each resume project you can speak at 3 depths (one-line → 1-min → 5-min versions)
- Your decision-replay says "if I were redoing this, here's what I'd change differently" (self-aware, not just bragging)
- Your reverse questions surprise the interviewer (not basic info, business depth)

## Stage 3: Cross-functional / technical deep-dive (30-60 min)

**Interviewer**: a peer from a different function (product/eng/design/data) or a technical specialist

**Mental model:**
- How deep is this person's knowledge of [my area]?
- How well do they collaborate across teams — could I work with them?
- Are technical decisions well-articulated (if they're PM) / business understanding sharp (if they're technical)?

**Most common questions:**
- **Domain fundamentals**: a specific technical concept / framework / algorithm in [role's core area]
- **Scenario design**: if we needed to build [project the JD implies], how would you design / decompose / validate it
- **Cross-team conflict**: have you had a conflict with engineering / design / data / how did you resolve it
- **Trade-off thinking**: how do you weigh X vs Y

**This stage measures "domain depth + cross-functional collaboration".** Prepare deep, multi-layer answers for the role's core domain (not surface-level knowledge).

**Signs you aced this stage:**
- You can answer in three layers (concept → practice → edge cases) for the role's core area
- Trade-off questions get "it depends: in setting A pick X, in setting B pick Y" (not "X because Y")
- Cross-team questions get concrete cases (not abstract "I'm a good communicator")

---

## Stage 4: Executive / final (30-45 min)

**Interviewer**: department head / VP / founder / cross-cutting senior

**Mental model:**
- Cultural fit?
- Can this person grow to operate independently over the long term?
- Do they have "operator perspective" (not just hands-on but thinking 1 yr / 3 yr ahead)?

**Most common questions:**
- **Why this company / why now**: why us over [competitor X]
- **5-year plan**: what do you want to be doing / be in 3-5 years
- **Values probes**: what are you proudest of / most regretful about / what does success mean to you
- **Scenario judgment**: suppose your project fails at month 6 after joining, how would you respond

**Soft signals are decisive here.** Technical detail is not pressed, but every sentence's "perspective / maturity / honesty" is being evaluated.

**Signs you aced this stage:**
- "Why this company" answer is not "your business is great" — it is grounded in a specific company strategy / a specific opportunity you have spotted
- 5-year plan is honest but directional (don't pretend "I don't know what I want")
- Reverse questions reach company-level strategy / pain / opportunity

---

## Pacing suggestion

**Preparation time differs by stage:**
- HR screen: 30 minutes (rehearse 1-min self-intro + nail salary anchor)
- Hiring manager: **2-3 hours** (this is the most important — every resume project must be 3-layer deep-explainable)
- Cross-functional / technical: 1-2 hours (target prep at the interviewer's area)
- Final: 1-2 hours (research company strategy / prep "why this company")

---

## Critical reminders

**Never do this at the end of an interview:**
- ❌ Ask on the spot "when will I hear back" (passive)
- ❌ Self-criticise "I didn't answer that one well"
- ❌ Forget to ask "what are the next steps / rough timeline" before leaving

**Always do this within 24 hours after each interview:**
- ✅ Write a retro: which questions went well / which stalled / how the interviewer reacted
- ✅ Send a thank-you note (email, 1-3 sentences) — mandatory for international companies, optional for big-tech in some markets
- ✅ Log a decision entry ("interviewed at X, predict moving to next round, review on YYYY-MM-DD")
