# Time and stage planning

Choose modules from today's explicit practice focus first, then the role and stage. These allocations never expand a focused session to the whole JD. Do not use company stereotypes. These are starting allocations, not quotas:

| Stage | Suggested assessment allocation |
|---|---|
| HR / recruiter | Experience and motivation 35%; role/experience verification 30%; behavioural judgment 20%; practical constraints 15% |
| Hiring manager | Relevant experience 10%; project/achievement depth 45%; role fundamentals 20%; scenario/decision judgment 25% |
| Technical specialist | Experience alignment 10%; project implementation 40%; fundamentals 25%; design/debugging/coding 25% |
| Senior / final | Experience and scope 15%; decisions/impact 35%; strategy/scenarios 30%; leadership/collaboration 20% |

Allocate 100% of assessment time to questioning. No mandatory self-introduction, reverse-question, recap, or coaching allocation. Merge small modules in short sessions. Adapt to nontechnical roles by replacing technical foundations with actual domain foundations.

For a 60-minute technical session, a plausible plan is 6 minutes alignment (about 1 main question), 24 minutes projects (about 2–3), 15 minutes foundations (about 3–4), 15 minutes scenarios (about 1–2). These ranges include follow-ups and depend on depth. Calculate minutes before counts; do not mechanically scale the same count to every session length.

Also distribute coverage across JD domains. For a backend-heavy full-stack role, backend/system/data requirements deserve protected time even when the resume foregrounds UI work. Domain weights follow the JD and level, not an equal split or a universal frontend/backend ratio.

At a module transition compare budget used, demonstrated evidence, gaps and untested priorities. Transfer time explicitly if justified; record original and revised allocations and the reason. Do not reserve an undisclosed extension. For a severely weak branch, establish the boundary, record a study gap, and sample another important requirement instead of teaching mid-interview.

Ask about salary, availability or location only when relevant to the simulated stage and user scope. Do not enforce blanket rules about what candidates may ask employers. Final reverse-question suggestions are outside assessment time.
