# Evidence-based evaluation

Keep evaluation private during simulation; deliver it after finish. Use observed answers, not the model answer alone. Quote or summarize the specific answer that supports each judgment. Fluency and JD keywords are not evidence of correctness.

Select dimensions by question type:

| Type | Dimensions |
|---|---|
| Fundamentals | Correctness; mechanism depth; boundary conditions; application; clarity |
| Project | Ownership accuracy; implementation depth; decision rationale; outcome evidence; reflection |
| Design/debugging/coding | Problem clarification; reasoning/correctness; trade-offs; validation; risk/edge cases |
| Behaviour/experience | Coherence; specificity; ownership; reflection; role relevance |

When useful, score applicable dimensions 0–4: 0 = demonstrated major error/no usable answer; 1 = fragments with substantive errors; 2 = broadly correct but important omissions; 3 = correct and supported at required depth; 4 = precise, robust reasoning including relevant boundaries. Mark N/A or untested rather than assigning zero without evidence. Report uncertainty when verification is pending. Do not deduct for missing numbers in a concept answer or missing STAR in a coding explanation. Do not infer spoken timing from typed text.

Coverage status is separate: untested, partial, demonstrated, gap, uncertain. A source not checking out makes the evaluation uncertain until resolved; it does not automatically make the candidate wrong. A confident wrong mechanism is more significant than imperfect wording. Version-dependent answers must be judged against the stated version and constraints.

Do not enforce pass-score loops or declare interview readiness/hiring probability from totals. Final evaluation should expose teachable gaps and the limits of what was observed.

Reference answers must answer the actual prompt plus every asked follow-up. Provide coherent, speakable explanations, and code/examples where necessary. For project answers separate candidate-supported facts from proposed approaches. Where personal facts are unknown, explicitly identify what the candidate must supply instead of fabricating a polished story. Never turn a suggested improvement into a claim the candidate actually implemented it.
