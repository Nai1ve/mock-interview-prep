# Company Types — Interview Style by Company Archetype

> The same role ("SEO Product Manager") gets very different interview questions depending on company type. This file helps the skill adapt question style and your answer emphasis to the company archetype.

---

## Type 1: Early-stage startup (< 100 people)

**Typical examples**: YC-backed companies, early-stage SaaS, AI startups
**Interviewer mindset**: can they go 0→1, self-direct, work full-stack, tolerate ambiguity

**They will always ask:**
- "Have you done a 0-to-1 project with no guidance?"
- "We don't have full SOPs — how do you figure things out?"
- "If your first-week brief from the boss is one sentence, how do you start?"

**Your emphasis in answers:**
- Strengthen "I led" / "I designed from zero" / "I figured it out myself"
- Reduce "team / process / cross-functional collaboration" (they don't have those)
- Proactively show "I wear multiple hats"

**Good reverse questions:**
- Company's biggest current challenge / runway situation
- How much decision authority does this role have
- Team culture / how conflicts get resolved

---

## Type 2: Domestic big-tech (e.g. ByteDance, Tencent, Alibaba, Meituan, Kuaishou, Didi, JD)

**Interviewer mindset**: can they fit the process, collaborate across teams, hit KPIs, NOT over-claim individual credit

**They will always ask:**
- "What exactly was your scope on X project" (probing ownership precision)
- "How do you collaborate with product / eng / design / data teams"
- "How do you trade off between boss demands — e.g. deadline vs quality"
- "How would you think about setting OKRs / quarterly goals"

**Your emphasis in answers:**
- Precise ownership ("I led X, the team handled Y" — don't over-claim)
- Concrete cross-team collaboration (specific roles + specific events)
- KPI thinking (quantify your deliverable)
- Avoid startup-style "I did everything" — big-tech reads this as chaotic

**Good reverse questions:**
- Team org structure / reporting line
- KPIs / OKRs for this role
- The team's biggest current cross-team project

**Special notes:**
- ByteDance ecosystem (CapCut / TikTok / Douyin): fast-paced, A/B-test driven, data-obsessed
- Kuaishou ecosystem (Kling): international business is now more strategically weighted than domestic
- Didi: international business restarting / emphasises localisation + AI
- Meituan: local services / O2O / fulfilment chain (if you don't understand fulfilment you'll struggle)

---

## Type 3: International big-tech (Google / Meta / Microsoft / Amazon)

**Interviewer mindset**: culture fit, leadership principles, English communication, long-term collaboration

**They will always ask:**
- Behavioural (full STAR framework, quantification-oriented)
- "Tell me about a time when..." series (leadership / conflict / failure / impact)
- Full-English interview (often including HR screen)
- "What's your 5-year plan" (these companies care about long-term fit)

**Your emphasis in answers:**
- **Full STAR** (most strict on structure)
- English fluency (does not need to be native, but complete sentences with natural pacing)
- Quantification-obsessed (every number with context)
- Leadership Principles (not just individual results — show "leading / driving / influencing")

**Good reverse questions:**
- Team culture / day-in-the-life
- Cross-regional collaboration (if distributed)
- Long-term growth path

**Special notes:**
- Amazon's 14 Leadership Principles must be memorised (Customer Obsession / Ownership / Bias for Action / …)
- Google's 4 evaluation dimensions (Cognitive Ability / Leadership / Role-Related Knowledge / Googleyness)
- Microsoft / Meta emphasise collaboration + impact + growth mindset

---

## Type 4: International SaaS startup (small, 30-200 people)

**Typical examples**: International PLG SaaS, AI tooling companies, content platforms
**Interviewer mindset**: pace + self-direction + cross-functional + English fluency

**They will always ask:**
- "Why this company and not [direct competitor X]" (tests whether you researched)
- "How would you approach [a specific business problem the JD implies]" (hands-on decomposition)
- "What KPIs would you set for this role" (ability to self-define KPIs)
- Full-English communication (mandatory)

**Your emphasis in answers:**
- Cadence (don't drag — pacing is a startup signal)
- "Why this company" must be specific (their product / users / competitor comparison)
- Proactively propose, don't just answer
- English fluency + pace matters more than formality

**Good reverse questions:**
- Recent funding / runway / growth targets
- Ownership boundary for this role
- The company's biggest recent product bet

## Company-type × Interview-stage matrix (key reminders)

| | HR screen | Hiring manager | Cross-functional | Final |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Early startup** | Short (15-20 min) | Deep on project + self-direction | Full-stack collaboration | Founder/CEO — culture + bet |
| **Domestic big-tech** | Standard (30 min) | Precise ownership on projects | Cross-team cases | Dept head — OKR thinking |
| **International big-tech** | Full-English STAR | Behavioural STAR | Bar Raiser / Cross-functional | Long-term / Leadership Principles |
| **International SaaS startup** | English + pacing | Hands-on decomposition | Cross-functional | Co-founder — fit |

---

## Skill usage

In Step 1 (context gathering), ask the user about company type:
- "Is the company a startup / domestic big-tech / international big-tech / international SaaS startup?"
- Use the answer to lookup this reference and tune Step 2 question distribution + Step 3 answer emphasis.

If the user does not specify, infer from company name (e.g. a 30-person company you don't recognise → startup; "Google" → international big-tech).
